# 01 — What Lesson 9 teaches

## 1. The lesson in one sentence

**A standing mark is a long vowel written without its letter.** Khari zabar is the alif of Lesson 8 made small and
set upright over the letter: بٰ is "baa", exactly as بَا is. Khari zair is the same idea below the letter, "bii";
ulta paish is paish turned over, "buu".

The student already knows two of the three ingredients: the short marks (lessons 4–6), and that a long vowel is the
short one stretched (Lesson 8). What is new is **reading length from the shape of a mark**, not from a letter
after it.

## 2. What "knowing it" is

A student knows this lesson when, shown بٰ بٖ بٗ in any order and on any letter, they:

1. **Tell each from its short twin** — بٰ from بَ, بٖ from بِ, بٗ from بُ. Same place, a different stroke: upright
   against slanted, and a curl turned the other way. This is the lesson's hard contrast and the one the warm-up
   parts drill.
2. **Tell the three apart** — which one is it? The last part's contrast, exactly as Lesson 7's last part asked
   "which two?".
3. **Say it long.** The part the drill cannot test without the teacher's voice (§4).

What it is **not**: knowing when the mushaf chooses a standing mark rather than a letter. That is spelling, not
reading, and a beginner never needs it — they read what is printed.

## 3. The contrast the drill must never ask

**بٰ against بَا.** The same sound, two spellings. In the name-it drill "Baa with khari zabar" against "Baa with
zabar and alif" would be a fair question about the picture — but the by-ear drill, which switches on the moment a
recording exists, would play "baa" and mark one of two right answers wrong. And the lesson's point is that they
*are* the same; a drill that treats them as different teaches the opposite.

So Lesson 8's item appears **on the board**, beside the khari zabar, as "the same sound" (`03` §7), and **never
among the answers**. Lesson 8's own plan said the same thing on 2026-09-27 (`docs/lesson-8/06` §5).

The other two have no Lesson 8 counterpart yet: بِي "bii" is Lesson 13 and بُو "buu" is Lesson 11. When those are
built, *they* show the standing mark as their same-sound tile — the comparison runs backwards, into this lesson.

## 4. Why the drill is the easy half again

As in Lesson 8 (`docs/lesson-8/07` §1), the drill tests the picture: "which mark is this?". The lesson is a sound.
But the picture is harder here than in Lesson 8 — an alif is a whole letter, and a standing mark is one small
stroke against another — so the drill is doing real work this time: **upright against slanted, on a tile**, is the
same kind of question as Lesson 6's zabar against paish, which the user found hard to see at the tile's first size.

The by-ear format costs **no new recordings** for khari zabar: its sound is Lesson 8's (`07` §6).

## 5. The shape of the lesson

Lesson 7's shape, unchanged — `SETS` and `partsOf` were written for this lesson as much as for tanween:

| Part | Mark | Letters | Wrong answers ride along from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | khari zabar | six (`02` §4) | zabar on the same letter |
| 2 | khari zair | six | zair on the same letter |
| 3 | ulta paish | six | paish on the same letter |
| 4 | all three | all 27, one mark each by alphabet place | the other two standing marks on the same letter |

Finishing is gated by part 4 alone, at seven tenths of 27, with nothing missed still shaky — the rule every drill
lesson has had since 2026-09-23.
