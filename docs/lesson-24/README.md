## What it teaches

A noon with a jazam (نْ), or a tanween, which ends in the same "n", is read **one of four ways, depending on the
next letter**:

| Plain name | Tajweed name | Before | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **said clearly** | izhar | ء ه ع ح غ خ (the throat letters) | a plain "n" | مِنْهُ, اَنْعَمْتَ |
| **merged** | idgham | ي ر م ل و ن | the "n" disappears into the next letter, which doubles; **with a hum** before ي ن م و, **without** before ل ر | مِنْ نَّارٍ, مِنْ رَّبِّهِمْ |
| **turned into meem** | iqlab | ب | an "m", with a hum | مِنْۢ بَعْدِ |
| **hidden** | ikhfa | the other fifteen letters | half-said, through the nose, a hum | مِنْ طِيْنٍ |

The hum (ghunna) is Lesson 15's, now with its full place.

## The two scripts: Madani prints the rule, Indo-Pak mostly doesn't

| Rule | Madani | Indo-Pak |
|---|---|---|
| said clearly | a sukun on the noon | a jazam on the noon |
| merged | **the noon bare**, a shadda on the next letter | **the jazam kept**, a shadda on the next letter (on a wow too: وَّلَهُمْ, which the Madani text does not mark) |
| turned into meem | **a small meem**, no sukun | the jazam **and** a small meem |
| hidden | **the noon bare** | **the jazam kept. Nothing marks it** |

So:
- **the Madani student reads it off the page**: sukun, say it clearly; bare, don't; small meem, "m";
- **the Indo-Pak student reads two of four off the page** (merged, turned) and has to **know** the rest: after a noon
  with jazam, a throat letter means clear, and any other letter with no shadda and no small meem means hidden. That
  is a short list (the six throat letters), not fifteen.

The board has one line per script (Lesson 11's mechanism). **This lesson is where the Madani and Indo-Pak students
learn the most different things.**

**The tanween gap:** the Madina mushaf also prints a *staggered* tanween for merged and hidden tanween, but Quran.com's
`text_uthmani` does not carry it (`docs/pass-2/01` §5). The Madani page can show the rule on noon from the text, but
for tanween it relies on the next letter, as the Indo-Pak one does. **To decide in the full plan:** fetch a different
Quran.com text field that carries it (its tajweed-coded or glyph fields), or teach tanween by the next letter in both
scripts. **Recommended: by the next letter.** Never add marks the fetched text does not have.

## What is drilled

| Part | Rule | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | said clearly | which way? (by ear first once recorded) |
| 2 | merged (with and without the hum) | which way? |
| 3 | turned into meem | which way? |
| 4 | hidden | which way? |
| 5 | all four, noon and tanween | which way? |

The choices are the four plain names. The picture question is honest for a Madani student (the marks differ) and
tests the letter list for an Indo-Pak one. **By ear it is the same question for both.**

## Examples (from the Qur'an, by reference; candidates)

Clear: مِنْهُ (7:12), اَنْعَمْتَ (1:7). Merged: مِنْ نَّارٍ (7:12), مِنْ رَّبِّهِمْ (2:5), نَّارٍ وَّخَلَقْتَهٗ (7:12, tanween
into wow). Turned: مِنْۢ بَعْدِ (2:27), اَلِيْمٌۢ بِمَا (2:10). Hidden: مِنْ طِيْنٍ (7:12), اَنْذَرْتَهُمْ (2:6, inside a word).
