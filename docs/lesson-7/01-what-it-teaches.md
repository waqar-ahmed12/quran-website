# 01 — What Lesson 7 teaches

## 1. The one sentence the lesson rests on

> **The mark written twice adds an *n* to the sound.**

That is the whole of it. A student who has finished lessons 4, 5 and 6 knows three marks and where each sits. This
lesson does not add a fourth stroke, a new place, or a new letter — it adds **a rule about doubling**, learnt once
and applied three times:

| Bare | One mark | Two marks | Says |
|---|---|---|---|
| ب | بَ | **بً** | ba → **ban** |
| ب | بِ | **بٍ** | bi → **bin** |
| ب | بُ | **بٌ** | bu → **bun** |

That is why Lesson 7 is one lesson and not three, and why it can carry three new characters where lessons 4–6
carried one each. The load is not three marks; it is one idea and three shapes that already look familiar.

## 2. What "knowing tanween" means here

The same four skills `docs/lesson-4/01` and `docs/lesson-5/01` set out, with one added:

| | Skill | Where it is drilled |
|---|---|---|
| A | The doubled mark is **there** at all | parts 1–3, against the bare letter (the `review` slider, off by default) |
| B | **One or two?** — بَ against بً | **parts 1–3**, where every question has the single mark as its twin |
| C | **Above or below?** — بٌ against بٍ | the last part, and free in parts 2 and 3 |
| D | **Which two?** — بً against بٌ | **the last part**, where the twins are the other tanweens (`03` §5) |
| E | The mark travels with the letter, wherever the letter sits | the board's joined example, shown and never drilled |

Skill **B** is the lesson. Skill **D** is what makes the last part a part and not a victory lap. If the build gets
only one of them right, get B right — a student who reads بً as "ba" is reading a word wrong; a student who reads
it as "bun" is reading a different word wrong, which is no worse and no better.

## 3. What it does **not** teach

- **No words.** Tanween only ever happens at the **end** of a word in real text, and a lesson that taught it in
  place would need words, which this pass does not have (`QAIDA-CONTENT.md`, "Scope for this pass"). Lesson 7 is
  the letter and the mark, the way lessons 4–6 are, and the way a printed Qaida's tanween table is.
- **No tajweed.** A tanween before certain letters is read with ikhfa, idghaam or iqlaab, and some mushafs write
  it differently to say so (`02` §5). That is a later pass, named as out of scope in `QAIDA-CONTENT.md`.
- **No case endings and no grammar.** Why a word ends in -un rather than -an is Arabic, not reading.
- **No "tanween means indefinite".** True, and useless to someone learning to read.

## 4. Why it comes here

`QAIDA-CONTENT.md` item 7, and a printed Qaida agrees: tanween sits immediately after the three harakaat because
it *is* the three harakaat. Every later lesson — madd, leen, standing harakaat, jazam — adds a letter or a
different stroke. This one adds only a repetition, so it is the cheapest possible consolidation of lessons 4–6,
and it doubles as their revision: a student who is shaky on paish will find out in part 3.

## 5. The honest risk in the pedagogy

**The doubling is a small visual difference carrying a large change of sound.** ب against بً is the difference
between "b", "ba" and "ban", and at a tile's size on a phone the difference between one stroke and two is a few
pixels. Lessons 4–6 could lean on *place* (above, below) and *shape* (a stroke, a curl). This lesson can lean only
on **count**.

Three things follow, and all three are in this specification:

1. The **tile size question is blocking** (`README`, `06` §0) — it is not a polish item here.
2. The **halo** (the ring that points at the mark) matters more than it did, because it is what says *look here,
   at this, not at the letter*. `03` §8 keeps it working when a lesson has more than one mark.
3. The board shows the family **side by side** — bare, single, doubled — so the student is comparing rather than
   remembering. `04` §3.

## 6. Finishing

Unchanged, and it is the user's rule from 2026-09-19: **a recommendation, never a gate.** Four fifths of the last
part's items known, with nothing missed still shaky, and the page says *you seem to know these*. Parts 1–3 are
warm-ups and are not the gate (`03` §3), so a confident student can go straight to the last part, and a student
who wants all four gets all four. Nothing is locked, here or anywhere.
