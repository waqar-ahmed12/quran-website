## What it teaches

A meem with a jazam (مْ) is read one of three ways, by the next letter:

| Plain name | Tajweed name | Before | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **merged** | idgham shafawi | another meem | قُلُوْبِهِمْ مَّرَضٌ: the meems become one, held with a hum |
| **hidden** | ikhfa shafawi | baa | تَرْمِيْهِمْ بِحِجَارَةٍ: the lips close lightly, a hum |
| **said clearly** | izhar shafawi | every other letter, **especially wow and faa** (where the lips want to run on) | وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ, عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ |

## The two scripts

The same pattern as noon (checked on 105:4 and 2:10):

| | Madani | Indo-Pak |
|---|---|---|
| merged | meem bare, shadda on the next meem | jazam kept, shadda on the next meem |
| hidden | **meem bare** | jazam kept, **nothing marks it** |
| clear | sukun on the meem | jazam |

The Indo-Pak student learns one letter: "a meem with jazam before baa is hidden".

## What is drilled

Three parts (one per rule) and a fourth mixing all three **and Lesson 24's noon**. Noon against meem, both with a
jazam, is exactly the confusion a reader has. It is the same "which way?" question with plain names, and by ear first
once recorded.

## Examples (from the Qur'an, by reference; candidates)
