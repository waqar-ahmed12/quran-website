# 01 — What Lesson 12 teaches

## 1. The one sentence

**Zabar, then a yaa with a jazam, says "ai"**: بَيْ "bai", تَيْ "tai", خَيْ "khai". The yaa is not read as a letter
with its own vowel (not "ba-yi"). It glides into the zabar, and the two make one sound.

A student who knows it can:
- read بَيْ on any of the 27 letters as one syllable;
- tell it from بَ (short, no yaa) and from **بَوْ** (Lesson 10: the same zabar, the same jazam, a different letter);
- read it inside a word: بَيْتٌ, خَيْرٌ, عَلَيْهِ.

## 2. The contrast

`against: ['fatha', 'fatha-waw']`, in the order taught. The quartet on the board is:

> ب → بَ → بَوْ → بَيْ

| Twin | Why |
|---|---|
| **بَ** (Lesson 4) | the short sound, so "bai" is never taken for "ba" with a stray letter |
| **بَوْ** (Lesson 10) | **the nearest contrast**: the same pattern (zabar, a letter, a jazam), and only the letter differs. "Bau" against "bai" |

Twins alternate per letter and flip between the parts (`data-twins="alternate"`).

**Not a twin: بَا** (Lesson 8, "baa"). It would be a third column and a fifth tile, and "baa" against "bai" is not a
contrast anyone confuses. `07` §3 asks.

**Not a twin: بِي** (Lesson 13, long "ee"). The student has not met it yet. Lesson 13 puts بَيْ against بِي, the
content plan's minimal pair, so that pairing is built from Lesson 13's side, as Lesson 11 did for the wow.

**Not a twin: بَيَ** (a yaa carrying its own zabar, "baya"). It is two syllables, and it belongs to Lesson 14.

## 3. What the drill can and cannot test

The same limit as Lesson 10 (`docs/lesson-10/01` §2): a student can answer the name-it drill by spotting *which
letter follows*, which they learnt in Lesson 1. The lesson is a sound, and only the by-ear format tests it. That
format switches on with the `fatha-yaa` recordings. The drill still makes the student look at the whole item every
time.

## 4. The jazam, named again

The student met the jazam on the wow in Lesson 10. The board's line (`04` §3) says it is the same mark doing the
same job on the yaa, and does not teach it again.

## 5. What is out

- **Tajweed**: leen's lengthening at a pause (خَيْرْ) is out of this pass.
- **A yaa with a shadda** (سَيِّدٌ، أَيَّامٌ). Shadda is a later pass.
- **Hamza after the yaa** (شَيْءٌ, "a thing"). ء sits on the line after a yaa that does not join it. It is a common
  word, but the two scripts' mushafs write it differently, and ء is left out of every item since Lesson 8.
