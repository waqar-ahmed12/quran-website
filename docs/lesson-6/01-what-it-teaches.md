# 01 — What Lesson 6 teaches

## 1. The skills, continued

`docs/lesson-4/01` named the skills a mark lesson builds, and `docs/lesson-5/01` added skill D. Lesson 6 adds one more.

| Skill | What the student can do | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| A | see that a letter carries a mark at all | Lesson 4 |
| B | name the letter under the mark | Lesson 4, again in 5 and 6 |
| C | say the sound the pair makes | Lesson 4 (by name until there are recordings) |
| D | tell a mark above from a mark below | Lesson 5 |
| **E** | **tell two marks apart by their shape**, when both sit above | **Lesson 6** |

Skill E is new, and it is harder than D. Seeing whether something is above or below the letter is easy. Seeing the
difference between a slanted line and a small curl, at the size a mark is printed, is a real skill. It is also the
one a beginner gets wrong in a real Qur'an for months.

It is also needed later. **Lesson 9** (standing marks) has an upside-down paish, **Lesson 7** (tanween) has two paish
side by side, and **Lesson 11** puts paish in front of a و. None of that is readable if the student is not sure what
a paish looks like.

## 2. What "knowing paish" means

A student knows paish when, shown بُ, they can say "Baa with paish", and not "Baa with zabar". Every paish question
has the zabar twin among its answers (`03` §3), so this is what the lesson actually tests, and the only way to
finish it.

Knowing it does **not** mean telling paish from zair. The student learnt that in Lesson 5. Zair still comes along
(`03` §2), but as review: it is never required, never counted, and never the thing the lesson is built on.

## 3. The three together

This is the first lesson where the student has all three short vowels. A printed Qaida marks that moment with a row
students chant: **بَ بِ بُ — ba, bi, bu**, then the next letter. The board should show exactly that row
(`04` §3). The drill tests it one mark at a time; the board shows all three side by side, which is where the
"these three belong together" idea is taught.

## 4. What stays the same as lessons 4 and 5

- **Two parts:** six letters to meet the mark on, then all 29. The six are Lesson 4's (`02` §3).
- **Answering by the pair's name** ("Baa with paish"). The by-ear format is built and turns itself on when a `damma`
  recording exists. There are none yet.
- **Only letters with marks.** No plain letters by default (the user, on Lesson 4: *"don't add simple alphabets
  without symbols"*). The slider stays in the options panel at 0.
- **No joined shapes drilled.** One new thing at a time.
- **Finishing is advice, never a lock.** Seven tenths of the 29 known, with nothing missed still shaky, then the
  Next button glows.

## 5. Mixed review, read properly

The user's rule (2026-09-19) is that every later lesson mixes in earlier material, and `QAIDA-CONTENT.md` item 6 says
Lesson 6's pool "mixes in zabar + zair review". Both come along here. What this plan adds is that they are **not
equal**: zabar is there because it is the wrong answer that teaches, and zair is there because the rule says review
reaches back. `03` §2 makes that difference real in the code.
