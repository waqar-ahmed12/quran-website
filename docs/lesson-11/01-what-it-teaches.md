# 01 — What Lesson 11 teaches

## 1. The one sentence

**Paish, then a wow, says a long "oo"**: بُو "buu", تُو "tuu", قُو "quu". The wow is not read as a letter with its
own vowel (not "bu-wa"). It stretches the paish, and the two make one long sound.

A student who knows it can:
- read بُو on any of the 27 letters as one long syllable;
- tell it from بُ (short "u", Lesson 6) and from بَوْ ("au", Lesson 10). **That second one is the minimal pair**;
- read it inside a word: نُورٌ, يَقُولُ, قُلُوبٌ.

## 2. The minimal pair

`QAIDA-CONTENT.md` item 11: *"Exercise should minimal-pair against #10 — same letter, hear one, pick which — since
wow is doing two different jobs one lesson apart."*

Two halves, and the build already has both:

1. **In the name-it drill (from the first day):** every question carries its twin on the same letter
   (`data-twins="alternate"`). About half the letters meet بَوْ as their wrong answer in part 1 and بُ in part 2,
   and the other half the other way round (Lesson 6's rule). The check proves every question offers its twin
   (`06` §3).
2. **By ear ("hear one, pick which"):** `SOUND_TO_MARK` plays the item's sound and asks the student to pick the
   picture. It switches on by itself, letter by letter, as the `damma-waw` recordings arrive. Those are the same 27
   recordings Lesson 9's ulta paish already asked for, so this lesson adds none. The choices then hold بُو and بَوْ on
   the same letter. That is exactly the content plan's "hear one, pick which", with **no new code**. `07` §3 asks
   whether this lesson should ask by ear *first* once the recordings exist.

## 3. The contrast

`against: ['damma', 'fatha-waw']`, **in the order taught** (Lesson 10's build settled this: the board reads the road,
and `{other}` is the short mark). The quartet on the board is:

> ب → بُ → بَوْ → بُو

| Twin | Why |
|---|---|
| **بُ** (Lesson 6) | the same paish, short: "bu" against "buu". The wow is what makes it long |
| **بَوْ** (Lesson 10) | the minimal pair: the same wow after a different mark. "Bau" against "buu" |

**Not a twin, and never an answer: بٗ (ulta paish, Lesson 9).** It is the same sound, "buu". The by-ear drill would
play "buu" and mark one of two right answers wrong (`docs/lesson-9/01` §3). It appears **beside** بُو as the
same-sound tile on part 1's feature row, the comparison Lesson 9 said would "run backwards, into this lesson".

**Not a twin: بُوَ** (a wow carrying its own vowel, "buwa"). It is two syllables and belongs with the jazam, in Lesson 14.

## 4. What the drill can and cannot test

The same honest limit as lessons 8 and 10: the name-it drill tests the picture, and the lesson is a sound. But this
time **the picture test is not the same in both scripts** (`README.md`):

- **Indo-Pak** (بَوْ against بُوْ): the only difference is zabar against paish. It sits in the same place and has a
  different shape. The student has to read the mark, which is real work. It is also the contrast the user found
  hard to see at the tile's first size (Lesson 6), so it goes first on the browser checklist (`06` §4).
- **Madani** (بَوْ against بُو): there are two differences, and a student can answer by "does the wow have a mark
  on it?" alone. That is a shortcut. It is honest to say so and not worth fighting: the Madani student is learning
  exactly the rule the Madani mushaf prints (a bare wow after paish is long).

## 5. What is out

- **Tajweed.** How long a long vowel is held (two counts, and longer before a hamza or a stop) is tajweed, and out of
  this pass (`QAIDA-CONTENT.md`, "Scope").
- **The silent alif after a plural wow** (قَالُوا "they said"). The alif is written and never read, which no lesson
  has taught. It is common, so `07` §6 puts it to the teacher. It is left out of the words (`05` §1).
- **Hamza on a seat** (أُو, and the ؤ of words like يُؤْمِنُونَ). It is not one of the 29.
- **A wow with a shadda** (قُوَّةٌ). Shadda is a later pass.
