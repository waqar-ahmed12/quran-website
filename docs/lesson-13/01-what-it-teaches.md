# 01 — What Lesson 13 teaches

## 1. The one sentence

**Zair, then a yaa, says a long "ee"**: بِي "bii", تِي "tii", فِي "fii". The yaa is not read as a letter with its
own vowel. It stretches the zair into one long sound.

A student who knows it can:
- read بِي on any of the 27 letters as one long syllable;
- tell it from بِ (short "i", Lesson 5) and from **بَيْ** ("ai", Lesson 12). **That second one is the minimal pair**;
- read it inside a word: فِيلٌ, دِينٌ, كَبِيرٌ.

## 2. The minimal pair, script by script

| | Lesson 12 | Lesson 13 | What tells them apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indo-Pak** | بَیْ | بِیْ | only the mark before the yaa: **above against below**, Lesson 5's contrast and the easiest one the Qaida has |
| **Madani** | بَيْ | بِي | the mark before the yaa, **and** whether the yaa carries a mark |

The name-it drill offers the twin in every question. The by-ear "hear one, pick which" switches on as the `kasra-yaa`
recordings arrive. Those are the 27 Lesson 9's khari zair already asked for, so this lesson adds none.
`docs/lesson-11/07` §3's "ask by ear first" question applies here too.

## 3. The contrast

`against: ['kasra', 'fatha-yaa']`, in the order taught. The quartet is:

> ب → بِ → بَيْ → بِي

**Not a twin, and never an answer: بٖ** (khari zair, Lesson 9, the same sound). It is shown beside بِي as the
same-sound tile on part 1 (Madani draws it بِۦ, zair and a small yaa). This is the second comparison Lesson 9 said
would "run backwards, into this lesson" (`docs/lesson-9/01` §3).

**Not a twin: بِيَ** (a yaa with its own vowel). That is Lesson 14.

## 4. What is out

The same as lessons 11 and 12: tajweed (how long the vowel is held), shadda (نَبِيٌّ), and hamza. Also left out:
**the word-final yaa of فِي "in"** as a *word* (`03` §2). It is the commonest long-"ee" word there is, and the item
already shows the end shape, but the Madani mushaf writes it dotless (فِى). Lesson 12's `07` §1 is the teacher's
answer on that.
