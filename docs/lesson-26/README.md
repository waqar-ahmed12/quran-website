## What it teaches

**Five letters, ق ط ب ج د, bounce when they have a jazam**: a small echo, so the letter is heard and not swallowed. The
bounce is **stronger at a stop**, when the jazam comes from stopping on the last letter (اَحَدْ at the end of 112:1).
The class calls it *qalqalah*, and its letters are remembered as قُطْبُ جَدٍّ.

Two strengths, one lesson:
- **inside a word or joined**: small (يَجْعَلُوْنَ, اَقْرَبُ);
- **at a stop**: clear (اَحَدْ, الْفَلَقْ, وَتَبّْ).

## The two scripts

**Neither prints it.** There is no mark for the bounce in either mushaf (checked on 112:1: اَحَدٌ carries nothing extra;
the full plan checks the other examples when they are fetched).
It is read off the letter and its jazam, so the rule is the five letters. It is the same for both scripts, and the
board says so.

## What is drilled

| Part | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | **tap the letter that bounces** in a word, where there is always exactly one |
| 2 | "does it bounce?" for a lit letter with a jazam (yes / no), the five letters against their neighbours |
| 3 | the stop: a verse end, which letter bounces when you stop there |

**By ear** is the real test and switches on with the recordings.

## Examples (from the Qur'an, by reference; candidates)

At a stop: اَحَدٌ (112:1), الصَّمَدُ (112:2), الْفَلَقِ (113:1), وَقَبَ (113:3), تَبَّ (111:1). Inside: وَلَمْ يُوْلَدْ (112:3), يَلِدْ
(112:3). A jazam on a letter that does *not* bounce, for "no": الْحَمْدُ (1:2), نَسْتَعِيْنُ (1:5).
