# 07 — Open questions

Eight questions. **None blocks the build** — each has a recommendation, and the build can start on those the way
lessons 4–9 did. §1 matters most; §2 is the one a teacher is most likely to answer differently.

## 0. Build now, or after a look at lessons 7–9?

**Recommended: a short look first — not a gate.** None of lessons 7, 8 or 9 has been seen by the user, and Lesson
10 is Lesson 8's tile, halo and joined block with a longer tail. If they are wrong on بَا, they are wrong on بَوْ.
If the user says build, build.

## 1. Show the jazam before Lesson 14?

| | What the student sees | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a. Show it, name it in one line** — *recommended* | بَوْ, and "the small mark on the wow is jazam…" | how every printed Qaida and every mushaf writes it; Lesson 14 then generalises something already seen | a mark before its lesson |
| b. Show it, say nothing | بَوْ | fewer words | a student will ask what the mark is, and the page has no answer |
| c. Leave it off | بَو | simpler to look at | a misspelling by the mushaf's rules; the student then meets بَوْ and has to relearn it |

## 2. The names

| | Zabar set (built on the home) | Fatha set |
|---|---|---|
| The lesson | Zabar and wow | Fatha and waw |
| The mark on the wow | **jazam** | **sukoon** |
| The sound's name | *not used* | *not used* |

"Leen" is what a Qaida class calls the sound, and it is the grammar-word kind of name the user asked to keep off
the page (`fixes/lesson 7/fixes.txt`). **Recommended: never say "leen"**, as Lesson 8 never says "madd". Would the
teacher say it?

## 3. Wow on wow (وَوْ)

In the table or not? **Recommended: in** — a printed Qaida's table has it, and it is honest reading practice. If
out, `skip` gains `'و'` and the lesson is 26.

## 4. بَوَ as a contrast

A wow carrying its own zabar ("bawa") against a wow with a jazam ("bau") is exactly what the jazam changes. **Recommended:
not here** — it is two syllables and belongs in Lesson 14. Built as a later option, it is one entry in `against`
once Lesson 14 has a way to name a two-syllable item.

## 5. More "apart" words

Only دَوْرٌ and زَوْجٌ show the wow standing apart (`05` §3). The teacher may know better short words (ذَوْ… رَوْ…)
without ة or a hamza.

## 6. "You'll meet jazam in Lesson 14"

A lesson number on the page, or "in a later lesson"? **Recommended: "in a later lesson"** — the page's other links
say titles, not numbers. One text field either way.

## 7. Lesson 7's words (from `docs/step-9/README.md` §0)

A real word ending in two zabar carries a silent alif after it (وَلَدًا), and the alif is Lesson 8. **Recommended:**
Lesson 7's walkthrough and reading page use two-paish and two-zair words only (قَلَمٌ "a pen", رَجُلٌ "a man",
بَلَدٍ "a town"…), with one line: "Words ending in two zabar come after Lesson 8, because they are written with an
alif." Or: include them, and let the alif be a first sighting, as the jazam is here.

## 8. Recording — a first word about Lesson 14

Lesson 10 needs **27 "bau" sounds** (`fatha-waw`), one sitting. Lesson 11 needs **none** (it is the same sound as
ulta paish, which Lesson 9 already asked for), nor does Lesson 13. So the order the teacher records in could be:
the 29 letter names → Lesson 4's "ba" → Lesson 8's "baa" → **Lesson 10's "bau"** → Lesson 12's "bai" → the rest.
For Lesson 14 (اَبْ, "ab"): would the teacher read a closed syllable on its own, or only inside a word? That decides
what Lesson 14's by-ear format can be.
