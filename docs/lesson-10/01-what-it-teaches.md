# 01 — What Lesson 10 teaches

## 1. The one sentence

**Zabar, then a wow with a jazam, says "au"** — بَوْ "bau", تَوْ "tau", قَوْ "qau". The wow is not read as a letter
with its own vowel (not "ba-wu"); it glides into the zabar and the two make one sound.

A student who knows it can:
- read بَوْ on any of the 27 letters as one syllable;
- tell it from بَ (short, no wow) and from بَا (Lesson 8, long "aa" — the same zabar, a different letter after it);
- read it inside a word: يَوْمٌ, خَوْفٌ, فَوْقَ.

## 2. What the drill can and cannot test

The same honest limit `docs/lesson-8/01` §2 wrote down: the name-it drill asks "Baa with zabar and wow" against
"Baa with zabar and alif", and a student can answer by spotting *which letter follows* — which they learnt in
Lesson 1. **The lesson is a sound**, and only the by-ear format (switched on when the `fatha-waw` recordings exist)
really tests it. The drill still earns its place: it makes the student look at the whole item every time, and it
fixes the shape "zabar, wow, jazam" in the eye before Lesson 11 puts بُو beside it.

## 3. The contrast

`against: ['fatha-alif', 'fatha']` — in that order, so the first twin (`otherOf`) is **بَا**:

| Twin | Why |
|---|---|
| **بَا** (Lesson 8) | the same shape — a zabar and a letter after it — so the student has to read the letter *and* hear the difference: "baa" and "bau" |
| **بَ** (Lesson 4) | the short sound, so "bau" is never mistaken for "ba" with a stray letter |

One twin per letter, alternating and flipping between the parts (`data-twins="alternate"`, Lesson 6's rule), so
twins stay near the 45% of questions Lesson 5 measured, not 60%.

**Not a twin: بُو.** It is Lesson 11 and the student has not met it. Lesson 11 puts بَوْ against بُو — the minimal
pair `QAIDA-CONTENT.md` item 11 asks for — so the pairing is built there, from that side.

**Not a twin, and never an answer: بَوَ** (a wow carrying its own zabar, "bawa"). It is a real and useful contrast —
it is exactly what the jazam changes — but it is two syllables and belongs to Lesson 14, where the jazam is taught.
`07` §4 asks whether the teacher wants it here anyway.

## 4. The jazam, named but not taught

The board says once what the small mark is (`04` §3): *"The small mark on the wow is jazam: the wow has no sound of
its own, so it runs into the zabar."* The drill never asks about it on its own, and nothing is scored on it. Lesson
14 generalises it to any letter.

## 5. What is out

- **Tajweed.** Leen's lengthening when a word stops on it (خَوْفْ at a pause) is tajweed and out of this pass
  (`QAIDA-CONTENT.md`, "Scope").
- **Hamza on a seat** (أَوْ "or", which a student will meet constantly). أ is not one of the 29, so it is not in the
  words (`05` §1), and ا/ء are not items (`02` §2).
