# 07 — Open questions

Seven questions, none blocking beyond `docs/pass-2/03` §1–2 (the pass itself). Each has a recommendation.

## 1. The names

| | Zabar set | Fatha set |
|---|---|---|
| The lesson | **Tashdeed** | **Shadda** |
| An item | "Baa with tashdeed and zabar" | "Baa with shadda and fatha" |

The zabar set is the Indo-Pak class's words (zabar, zair, paish, jazam), and an Indo-Pak class says *tashdeed*. The
fatha set is the Arabic one, where it is *shadda*. **Recommended as in the table.** Does the teacher say "tashdeed"?

## 2. One lead or matching leads?

| | The table | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **اَ everywhere** — *recommended* | اَبَّ اَبِّ اَبُّ | the same lead as Lesson 14, so one thing changes at a time; رَبِّ (a zabar before a zair shadda) is the Qur'an's commonest shape | not the printed table's |
| Matching | اَبَّ اِبِّ اُبُّ | a printed Qaida's own table | three leads to read past, and the student's eye lands on the wrong letter |

One field per row (`lead`), so the teacher can try both in the options panel if a row is added for it.

## 3. The kasra with shadda in Madani

If `02` §3 finds the Madani face putting the kasra under the letter (Indo-Pak style), switch it with the face's own
feature (recommended), or leave both scripts drawing it under the letter? **Recommended: follow the Madina mushaf.**
A Madani reader's first page of the Qur'an will have it under the shadda, and should have seen it here first.

## 4. The recordings: 81, or only the items?

The recordings page lists every letter for each of the three vowels, **81 rows**. The lesson's items use about 27 + 18
of them. **Recommended: record the 27 zabar ones first** (part 1 and much of part 4, "ab-ba, at-ta…"). The page
already counts what is recorded and says so honestly. With noon and meem, **record the hum as you would read it**.

## 5. The hum: named, or drilled?

**Recommended: named once, not drilled** (`01` §4). The drill cannot hear it. Lesson 24 teaches the hum properly, with
the noon rules.

## 6. Shadda with two zabar / zair / paish

كُلٌّ "all", حَقٌّ "truth", عَدُوٌّ "an enemy" are common. They need three more rows (a shadda with each doubled mark),
and they teach nothing new: "said twice" plus Lesson 7. **Recommended: not in this lesson.** They are in the Qur'an
examples from Lesson 18 on, where the words are copied and need no rows.

## 7. The home in two parts

"Learning to read" (1–14) and "Reading the Qur'an" (15–29)? Other titles, or one long list? **Recommended: two parts**,
because a student who has finished the first sees clearly that they have finished something. The finish screen (step 11)
still comes at the very end.
