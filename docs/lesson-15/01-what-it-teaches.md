# 01 — What Lesson 15 teaches

## 1. The one sentence

**A shadda means the letter is said twice: first with no vowel, closing the sound before it, then with its own
vowel.** اَبَّ is "ab-ba". The first b closes "ab" and the second opens "ba".

A student who knows it can:
- read اَبَّ اَبِّ اَبُّ on any of 27 letters, as two syllables sharing the letter;
- **tell اَبَّ from اَبَ ("a-ba", the letter once, with a vowel) and from اَبْ ("ab", the letter once, closed)**. The
  shadda is both of those at once;
- tell which vowel rides on the shadda (اَبَّ, اَبِّ, اَبُّ);
- read it inside a word: مَرَّ, عَلَّمَ, مُحَمَّدٌ;
- hold نّ and مّ with a hum.

## 2. The shape: Lesson 7's, with Lesson 14's lead

| Part | Marks | Letters | Wrong answers ride along from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | shadda with zabar, اَبَّ | six (zabar's own: ب د ر س م ل) | the same letter with zabar only (اَبَ) or jazam only (اَبْ), alternating |
| 2 | shadda with zair, اَبِّ | six (khari zair's: ه د ت ط ك ف) | اَبِ or اَبْ |
| 3 | shadda with paish, اَبُّ | six (paish's own: ب د ر س م ل) | اَبُ or اَبْ |
| 4 | all three | all 27, one vowel each by alphabet place | the other two shaddas on the same letter: "which vowel?" |

Finishing is gated by part 4, at seven tenths of 27, with nothing missed still shaky (the rule since 2026-09-23).

**Every item is drawn after اَ**, Lesson 14's lead (`docs/lesson-14/02` §2), for the same reason: the first half of a
shadda closes the syllable before it, so there has to be one. A printed Qaida's shadda table is built the same way.

## 3. The board: the shadda as a sum

In the warm-ups the quartet reads **ب → اَبَ → اَبْ → اَبَّ**: the letter; once, with a vowel; once, closed; and both
at once. The three captions say it: "a-ba", "ab", "ab-ba". It is the clearest picture of what a shadda *is* that the
page can draw, and every tile is one the student has already read, except the last.

## 4. The hum (ghunna), named once

On **ن** and **م** a shadda is held for a moment with a hum through the nose: اَنَّ, اَمَّ, ثُمَّ, اِنَّ. It is tajweed,
but it happens on every نّ and مّ in the Qur'an, and the student will hear it in the teacher's recording of the item
itself. **One line on the board**, shown when the open part has ن or م in it (`04` §3). It is named once
("this is called *ghunna*") and not drilled: the drill cannot hear a hum. Lesson 24 returns to it.

## 5. What the drill can and cannot test

The picture test is honest this time. The student has to see whether there is a shadda, and which vowel is on it
(and in Indo-Pak, whether the kasra under a shadda is the kasra under the letter, `02` §3). **The sound, two
syllables sharing a letter, and the hum, are tested only by ear**, with the teacher's recordings (`07` §4).

## 6. What is out

- **The article's shadda** (الرَّحْمٰنِ, ٱلشَّمْسُ): the laam that is not read and the next letter doubled. That is
  Lesson 18, where it is the whole lesson.
- **A shadda at the start of a word** (the same thing, after the article). It is never in this lesson's words
  (`03` §6).
- **A shadda with two zabar/zair/paish** (كُلٌّ, حَقٌّ) and **with a long vowel** (الضَّآلِّيْنَ). They are in the
  Qur'an examples from Lesson 18 on. Here they would need rows of their own, for no new idea (`07` §6).
- **Two shaddas in a row across a word** (the الضَّآلِّيْنَ kind), which is also Lesson 18's and 21's.
