What I did find are the underlying techniques your [veo-prompt.md](videos/veo-prompt.md) already uses, plus a couple of ways to tighten it:

**1. First/last-frame interpolation is the officially-recommended method for exactly this** — Google's own Veo 3.1 docs and Curious Refuge both describe pinning both ends as the standard way to do a controlled object transform (tight-shot → wide reveal, or closed → open), which is precisely the "Frames to Video" approach you already switched to for attempt 2.

**2. A tighter recipe for the static-camera language** (from Creatide's guide): structure it as three explicit parts rather than one long paragraph —
   - one sentence stating the camera is entirely motionless
   - one sentence assigning *all* motion to the subject only ("the cover opens; nothing else moves")
   - never combine "static" with any camera verb (pan/zoom/tilt) in the same prompt — mixing them is what causes models to drift

   Your current prompt already does this instinctively but spreads it across several paragraphs — could be worth consolidating into three tight sentences up front, since some models weight early instructions more heavily.

**3. General structure confirmation** (Google Cloud's Veo 3.1 guide): Subject → Action → Scene → Camera → Lighting → Style, in that order — matches what you have.

Nothing out there is a plug-and-play replacement for your hand-written prompt — if anything, the research confirms attempt 2's approach (locked camera, first+last frame pinning, explicit "only the cover moves") is already the correct strategy for this exact failure mode. The one concrete change worth considering: front-loading the "camera motionless / only cover moves" instructions as the first two sentences instead of the fourth paragraph.
